Hello everyone! Sorry for the delayed update. I hope most of you saw my Facebook post announcing that we officially took custody of our son, Lewis, last week! The past few days have been a blissful whirlwind. We're soaking up all our time with Lewis and feeling so grateful to be back home together as a family!
<br/>
![lewis](/images/lewis-1.jpg)
<br/>
Before I update you on what's been happening, let me formally introduce you to Lewis Gyu Seong Ng! Lewis is 22 months old and will be turning two on December 16th! He spent his first years living in Seoul, South Korea, with an amazing foster family who showed him great love and care. As of September 30th, 2024, he is officially an Ng! As you'll see in pictures, and when you meet him, he's the happiest little guy who loves to laugh and play. He was given the name Gyu Seong at birth. When were deciding what we would call him, we knew we wanted to keep part of his Korean name and also wanted to give him an English name. We thought through a few names and ultimately decided on Lewis. It's a family name for me, as it's my dad's middle name and was also used by my great and great-great-grandfathers. His full name combines three of the cultures that will shape him as he grows: American, Korean, and Cantonese 💜.
<br/>
Now that you know a bit about Lewis, let me update you on our lives over the past few weeks. We had our custody meeting on Monday, September 30th. We went to the adoption agency's office and met with his foster family one last time. Tommy and I were feeling all the emotions as we made our way to the agency, excited and a little anxious as we were unsure of how things would go. We'd been told to prepare for an emotional goodbye, as it would be hard for the foster family. I wanted to be sure to show my love and gratitude while also being a steady support for Lewis.
<br/>
When we arrived at the agency, we first signed some documents stating our official custody of Lewis. Then we headed upstairs, where Lewis and his foster family were waiting for us. Lewis was his usual happy self, excited to see us and eager to play like we normally do in that setting, pretty much completely unaware of how big of a day this would be for him. We had about an hour before the taxi we'd scheduled to pick us up would arrive. The foster family gave us a few big bags with Lewis's belongings, including his favorite toys, clothes, and food. The foster mom explained all the items she had packed for us and gave us some final tips on Lewis's routines etc. Then we gave the foster family and the Holt staff some gifts to thank them for all their help during our time in Korea and with Lewis over the past few years.
<br/>
We spent the next 20-30 minutes playing with Lewis and watching him run around. He's such a happy kid, which helped make the transition easier and eased some of the tension. After what felt like maybe a little too long of playing and delaying the goodbyes, it was time to leave. As we headed to the elevator, Lewis was in his foster dad's arms, and he reached out to me. It felt like he knew exactly what both his foster family and his new family needed in that moment—the reassurance that he was going to the perfect home for him 💛.
<br/>
When we got downstairs, our driver was waiting, along with what felt like 15 other foster moms who wanted to say goodbye. The foster mother network in Korea is very strong, and many of them are friends who will miss Lewis just as much as his foster family. After a few more photos and goodbyes, we got into the van, with Lewis hugging me and laying on my lap as we drove away.
<br/>
The drive back to our Airbnb took about thirty minutes. Lewis was quiet and somber the entire ride, though he didn't cry once. Our driver, who used to work for the adoption agency and has transported many adoptive families, was surprised. While I was glad Lewis wasn't crying, it was still difficult to see him so subdued, as he's normally such a happy, playful kid. But as we got closer to the Airbnb, he started playing with Tommy's hat, throwing it on the ground, which quickly brought back his laughter and cheerful self.
<br/>
We spent the rest of the day settling in, and the next day was free for us to do whatever we liked. On Wednesday, we had our visa appointment at the US Embassy, and on Friday, we headed home! We spent some of our free time before heading home getting out and exploring a bit, visiting some nearby parks and making a trip to the aquarium. Our time in Korea went very smoothly, and while we were ready to get back home and settle in, we were grateful for the quiet bonding time with just the three of us before coming back to our hectic lives in the US. Lewis slept quite well during those first nights in Korea, which was refreshing and allowed Tommy and I to get some good rest before our long flight on Friday.
<br/>

<br/>
Our flight on Friday was at 4 p.m., so we spent the morning packing and took a short walk to tire Lewis out before heading to the airport. We arrived early, giving us time to walk him around and burn off more energy before boarding. We were fortunate to have a direct flight from Seoul to San Francisco, so we only had to get through one 10-hour flight.
<br/>
![Flight home](/images/airplane-pic.jpg)
<br/>
Lewis charmed the flight attendants and passengers with his smile and laugh. After the first meal service, we changed him into pajamas, and he fell asleep on my lap. After a few hours, I had to use the restroom, so he slept on Tommy for a bit before waking up and realizing he was on a plane 😅. It took a while to get him back to sleep, but eventually, he dozed off and stayed asleep until we landed—as long as I didn't move much, haha. Overall, the flight went as smoothly as we could have hoped, with no major meltdowns and just the fussiness you'd expect from a 1-year-old on his first flight after a big life transition.
<br/>
It feels so good to be home and together as a family. Lewis is loving his new home, especially his big sister and our dog! We've spent the last few days settling in and trying to establish a sleep routine. He slept well in Korea, but the new environment and jet lag have thrown things off. Hopefully, we can nail down a routine soon and get over the jet lag ourselves. Tommy and I are lucky to have a good amount of parental time off work so we're really trying to make the most of it and get settled in well.
